# Level 10 / Level 11 project rules

Use first principles for environmental changes: establish metre-scale road cross-sections, lot boundaries, pedestrian routes, drainage, building footprints and light direction before adding visual detail. Think through multiple spatial solutions, choose one that makes physical sense, and inspect its joins with neighboring generated blocks.

The user explicitly prioritizes rich, plausible streets rather than isolated buildings on empty asphalt. New ordinary city blocks need continuous street walls, deliberate service alleys, complete sidewalks, grounded furniture, readable shop interiors and view-dependent window reflections. A high asset count is useful only when those assets fit the building's use and scale.

The two authored photographic landmarks are protected by the latest user instruction: preserve their building geometry, camera composition and weather unless the user reopens that scope. Shopfront imagery may be improved. Adjacent paving and ordinary infill must accommodate the protected footprints, steps, doors and drains without clipping or concealing them.

For visual fixes, inspect real rendered output as well as code. Verify visible pavement against walking height, check asset completeness and shader compilation, and label offscreen diagnostics honestly. Never describe a software diagnostic as a browser or live-site screenshot.

Large image libraries must contain independently created assets, not recolors counted as new scenes. Preserve asset provenance. Site source and publishing remain owned by the primary site agent; delegated asset work stays outside the checkout until integrated.

New environmental textures use believable PS2-era photographic diffuse surfaces: restrained softness, natural material grain and mild wear, seamless metre-scale tiling where relevant. Avoid pixel-art filters, modern oversharpening and excessive decay. Commercial advertisements are individually image-generated period artwork; code may pack the assets but must not substitute procedural graphic designs.

Ordinary facade fixtures share an explicit occupancy plan. Reserve glazing, fascia, canopy, doorway and fire-escape clearances before adding AC units, advertisements and projecting signs. Large banners require an intentionally blank wall; rooftop boards require anchored supports and space reserved from HVAC.

When reviewing landmark integration, inspect the side and rear voids from the user’s problem viewpoints as well as the authored front camera. Surface ownership must be explicit at the rural/approach/reference/procedural joins; do not use tiny height offsets to hide overlapping ground. Keep the clinic courtyard → substation passage → fountain plaza pedestrian route open.
